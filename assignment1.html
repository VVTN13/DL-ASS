Problem statement

We classify a single 28×28 grayscale clothing image into one of 10 product categories (T-shirt/top, Trouser, Pullover, Dress, Coat, Sandal, Shirt, Sneaker, Bag, Ankle boot). The same kind of task appears in retail catalogues, where product photos need to be sorted automatically.

  • Input: one 28×28 grayscale image, raw uint8 pixels 0–255.
  • Output: 10 class logits, and the predicted label is the argmax.
  • Prediction unit: one image.
  • Main difficulties: low resolution and no colour; upper-body garments (T-shirt, Pullover, Coat, Shirt) have very similar silhouettes; there is noticeable variation within each class.

Dataset description and EDA

Fashion-MNIST (Zalando, MIT license) is the main comparison dataset. We load it with torchvision.datasets.FashionMNIST. It has 60,000 official training images and 10,000 official test images. MNIST is used only for debugging.

  • The split uses seed 42 and is stored in split_cache.json. Train and validation indices do not overlap, and no exact duplicate images appear in both.
  • The classes are perfectly balanced: the max/min ratio is 1.000 and each class is 10% of every subset.
  • Training pixels have a mean of 72.98/255 and a standard deviation of 90.06/255. 50.2% of pixels are zero, and no image is entirely blank.
  • Mean brightness depends strongly on the class, from about 34.9 for Sandal to 98.1 for Coat, and so does foreground coverage.

Comparison and discussion

The MLP scores about 3.4 points higher than the linear baseline in both validation accuracy (0.8987 vs 0.8648) and macro-F1 (0.8985 vs 0.8641). It has about 30× more parameters, but training time is almost the same (about 28 s for 10 epochs on MPS). The linear model's validation loss levels off at about 0.39 by epoch 5, so it is limited by capacity. The MLP's training loss keeps falling after its validation loss bottoms out at epoch 8, which is an early sign of overfitting. With only one seed, these differences are preliminary.

Limitations and conclusion

  • We have only one seed and one validation split, and we have not measured variance yet.
  • The official test set has not been evaluated yet (planned for M2).
  • The M1 models use flattened pixels and ignore spatial structure. The CNN, sequence, and Transformer models in M2 will address this.
