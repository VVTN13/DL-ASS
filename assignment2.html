Assignment 2 — Five-Stage Sleep Classification from EEG

Group G-09 · CC01 · Final full-cohort results

Comparing compact waveform models and a pretrained EEG foundation model on participant-separated sleep recordings.

Final report (PDF) · Watch the presentation · Test results · Reproduction guide

Group and problem

Lê Phước Vũ (2353341) · Nguyễn Vũ Long (2352696) · Nguyễn Hoàng Quốc (2353027).

Predict W, N1, N2, N3 or REM from a labelled 30-second EEG epoch. The study examines classification quality, minority-stage errors and computational cost. It is a retrospective benchmark, not a clinically validated scoring service.

The Sleep-EDF topic was approved without conditions; see the proposal and approval record. The final study uses the full eligible cohort; the earlier 6,474-epoch subset was a pilot.

Data and exploratory analysis

Sleep-EDF Expanded, Sleep Cassette: 78 participants, 153 recordings and 195,469 eligible epochs. Development uses 66 participants and 170,537 epochs; held-out testing uses 12 participants and 24,932 epochs. A participant's nights remain together.

Preparation and models

Continuous Fpz-Cz and Pz-Oz signals are bandpass filtered at 0.3–35 Hz and resampled from 100 to 200 Hz. Each epoch becomes a (2, 30, 200) tensor in microvolts / 100. Stages 3 and 4 merge into N3, movement/unscored intervals are excluded, and wake is retained within 30 minutes around scored sleep. The tensor contains waveform samples, not a spectrogram.

Evaluation protocol

Five fixed participant-grouped development folds, seed 42, weighted cross-entropy with training-only class weights, Adam/AMSGrad, and up to 80 epochs. Checkpoints maximize validation macro-F1; early stopping uses patience 10 and minimum improvement 0.001. JSON configurations specify each model's learning rate, batch size and scheduler.

  • Fold mean ± SD: variability across five validation partitions at their selected checkpoints.
  • Pooled validation: every development epoch predicted once by its own validation-fold model.
  • Held-out test: average the five models' probabilities, then take argmax for the separate test participants.

Validation influences model selection and is not independent test evidence. Balanced accuracy equals macro-recall here. MGm is the mean of five one-vs-rest square roots of specificity × recall.

Compute cost and controlled comparison

Logged training-plus-validation totals on RTX 3090 are 2.369 hours for CNN, 3.399 hours for Transformer, 1.241 hours for the completed AttnSleep rerun and 3.360 hours for CBraMod. Batch sizes and cloud host conditions differ; these are observed costs, not matched throughput benchmarks. The report provides a separate, explicitly labelled CPU inference benchmark.

The preparation-recipe sensitivity study retains its original paired AttnSleep experiments, separate from the new main rerun. Common versus native-rate preparation gives validation macro-F1 0.7441 versus 0.7407, and test macro-F1 0.750965 versus 0.750480. The native recipe retains ten additional development Wake epochs. This compares whole recipes and does not isolate filtering, prove equivalence or reproduce the author's 20-fold benchmark.

A separate inference-only ensemble ablation evaluates all five saved Transformer checkpoints on the same 24,932 test epochs in CPU float32. Mean individual macro-F1 is 0.743078 versus 0.756452 for equal-weight soft voting: a 1.34-percentage-point improvement, requiring five forward passes. The paired participant-bootstrap 95% interval for the improvement is 1.01–1.66 points, conditional on the fixed checkpoints. This post-hoc analysis does not retrain models or choose a fold using test scores. The CPU ensemble differs from the archived CUDA labels on three epochs; the main comparison above retains the original result. Ablation evidence.

Error analysis and limitations

N1 is the weakest class for every model. AttnSleep's largest directed error is N2 predicted as N1 (932 epochs). The Transformer often confuses W with N1 and N2 with N1; its 46 fewer total errors than AttnSleep are modest on this correlated test cohort.

The report links held-out waveforms to confident correct, low-margin correct and confident incorrect Transformer predictions. A paired participant bootstrap supports the observed Transformer improvement over this CNN baseline, conditional on the trained models and twelve test participants.

Single-cohort data, annotation-informed trimming, legacy labels, repeated validation choices, one seed stream and model-specific inputs constrain generalization. Recorded cloud and local AttnSleep dataset serializations have different hashes: all sample identities and preprocessing metadata match, and a 128-example CPU check reproduces all saved labels; this does not prove bitwise waveform equality. Historical cloud Git commits and matched GPU latency/peak-memory measurements remain unavailable.
