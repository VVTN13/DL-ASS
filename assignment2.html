Problem statement

Given a non-overlapping 30-second EEG epoch, predict one of five sleep stages: wake (W), N1, N2, N3, or REM. The question is whether fine-tuned CBraMod improves stage-balanced performance over a compact CNN under the same sampled-data and participant-separated evaluation protocol.

  • Input: Fpz-Cz and Pz-Oz EEG channels, originally sampled at 100 Hz; preprocessing produces a (2, 30, 200) waveform tensor.
  • Output: five logits; argmax gives the predicted stage, and softmax probabilities support AUROC calculation.
  • Challenges: class imbalance, variation between participants, ambiguous sleep-stage boundaries, and a limited training budget.
  • Scope: retrospective sleep staging, not sleep-disorder diagnosis or a clinically validated system.

Dataset description and EDA

Sleep-EDF Database Expanded, version 1.0.0 (DOI 10.13026/C2X676) is distributed under the Open Data Commons Attribution License v1.0. We use the Sleep Cassette cohort: 78 participants and 153 recordings, excluding Sleep Telemetry.

Legacy stages 3 and 4 are merged into N3; movement and unscored intervals are excluded. Complete epochs are retained from 30 minutes before the first scored sleep interval to 30 minutes after the last, within recording boundaries. This label-dependent trimming defines a retrospective benchmark.

Methodology

  1. Pair EDF recordings with expert hypnograms; check channels, timing and epoch boundaries; apply the stage mapping, wake margins and participant sampling.
  2. Filter continuous EEG with a zero-phase 0.3–35 Hz FIR bandpass, then resample from 100 to 200 Hz. Upsampling matches the model interface; it does not add measured high-frequency information.
  3. Scale volts into units of 100 µV by multiplying by 104. Reshape each channel's 6,000 samples into 30 one-second patches, producing (2, 30, 200).
  4. Save waveforms, labels, participant/recording IDs, onsets and provenance. Memory-map the tensor and expose only development epochs to the training dataset.
  5. Train each model with unweighted multiclass cross-entropy. The current input transform is an identity: no extra z-score normalization, augmentation or temporal smoothing is applied.

The waveform grid's axes are seconds and within-second samples, not time and frequency. All current models receive this same representation.

Experimental setup

  • Holdout: 66 development participants (5,478 epochs) and 12 reserved test participants (996 epochs). All nights and epochs from a participant stay together.
  • Cross-validation: five-fold StratifiedGroupKFold, shuffled with seed 42, regenerated from sampled development labels. These are not the original full-cohort fold assignments. Folds 1–4 each use 4,399 training / 1,079 validation epochs; fold 5 uses 4,316 / 1,162.
  • Shared budget: batch size 32, at most 80 epochs, new model and optimizer per fold, NumPy/PyTorch seeds 42, cosine scheduling with T_max=100 and minimum learning rate 10−6.
  • Recorded checkpoint selection: highest validation macro one-vs-rest AUROC. Early stopping separately monitors validation loss with patience 10 and minimum improvement 0.001. Macro-F1-based selection remains an alternative to evaluate.
  • Hardware evidence: saved runs report CUDA use, with AMP enabled on CUDA. GPU identity, peak memory and a final runtime environment snapshot were not recorded.

Compute-cost evidence

Across five folds, logged epoch timers sum to 79.44 s for StandardCNN2D (132 epochs), 713.42 s for the main CBraMod run (127 epochs), and 305.79 s for ResCBAM (187 epochs). These include training, validation and metric calculation, but exclude setup, model loading, plotting and preprocessing. Without matched hardware records, they are provisional observations, not a controlled speed benchmark. The sampled waveform export is approximately 311 MB.

Comparison and discussion

The main CBraMod configuration improves recorded accuracy by 5.85 percentage points and macro-F1 by 8.20 percentage points over StandardCNN2D. This supports the comparison hypothesis for these configurations, but does not establish statistical significance or isolate pretraining from differences in architecture and capacity.

The higher CBraMod learning rate improves mean macro-F1 by 4.65 percentage points over the lower-rate run. This is a preliminary factor comparison: seeds were set once before cross-validation, so different stopping lengths can change later folds' initialization and data order. ResCBAM does not outperform the simpler CNN here, and its different optimizer settings prevent attributing the result to attention alone.

Training and validation curves in the report are consistent with overfitting. An AUROC-selected epoch need not maximize macro-F1. Controlled AUROC-versus-macro-F1 checkpoint selection and frozen-backbone-versus-full-fine-tuning ablations remain planned work, not completed results.

Limitations and conclusion

The accepted subset preserves participant coverage but sacrifices temporal continuity and within-night coverage. Two EEG channels, legacy labels, label-dependent trimming and sampling constrain generalization. Zero-phase filtering also means this is not evidence of causal online inference. Validation reuse, a single seed stream, incomplete runtime/checkpoint provenance and unrecorded hardware limit the strength of the comparison.

M2 conclusion: the waveform pipeline and grouped validation establish a working baseline and a stronger preliminary CBraMod result. N1 recognition remains a major weakness, and final test performance is unknown.

Next steps for M3 are to archive exact fold memberships and trained weights, compare checkpoint-selection rules and frozen versus fine-tuned CBraMod, complete prediction-level error analysis and matched-hardware measurements, then refit the selected configuration on development data and evaluate the reserved participants only after decisions are fixed.
